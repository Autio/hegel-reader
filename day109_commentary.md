**Book Three: The Doctrine of the Concept — §§1351-1355 (approx.)**

---

### § The Reading

Hegel begins the **Judgment** (*Urteil*) with **The Categorical Judgment**. The judgment is the Concept's *self-particularization* — the universal *splits* itself into subject and predicate.

"The genus essentially divides or repels itself into species; it is genus only in so far as it comprehends the species under it; the species is a species only in so far as, in the genus, it is related to its other species."

Translation: The universal (genus) *repels* itself into particulars (species). The judgment "S is P" is the universal *positing* its own particularity. The subject = the universal as particularized; the predicate = the universal's content.

Key: **The judgment is not "subject HAS predicate" — it's the universal PARTICULARIZING itself.** The subject is the universal *as individual* (this particular rose); the predicate is the universal *as content* (red). The copula "is" = the identity of the two as moments of the same universal.

This is Hegel's revolution in logic: the judgment isn't a relation between two independent things (subject and predicate); it's the *Concept splitting itself* and immediately reuniting itself.

---

### 🔍 Coherence Evaluation

This is Hegel's rethinking of the judgment. Traditional logic: judgment = "S is P" where S and P are independent, connected by the mind. Hegel: judgment = the Concept *itself* differentiating into subject and predicate, then recognizing their identity.

**Strength:** This dissolves the subject/predicate dualism. The predicate isn't "added to" the subject — the subject *already contains* the predicate as its own universal content. "The rose is red" = the rose (particular) expresses its universal nature (redness).

**Tension:** But then what distinguishes *true* judgments from *false* ones? If every judgment is the Concept self-particularizing, how can a judgment be wrong? Hegel will address this in the hypothetical and disjunctive judgments.

**Connection to Syllogism:** The categorical judgment is the *premise* of the syllogism. The syllogism mediates the universal through the particular into the individual — the judgment is the first step of that mediation.
